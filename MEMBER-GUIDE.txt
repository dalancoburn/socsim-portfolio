SOCSIM MEMBER PORTFOLIO — SETUP AND USAGE GUIDE
The SOC Simulation Project · skool.com/socsim/about
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════


WHAT IS THIS?
─────────────
Your portfolio is a personal website where you document every
investigation and BEC case drop you complete in the SOCSIM
lab. It shows the work behind the cert — your methodology, your
findings, your tooling.

You fill it in through a form-based editor. No coding required.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 1 — FIRST-TIME SETUP (do this once)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

STEP 1 — Create your portfolio repo
  1. Go to: github.com/dalancoburn/socsim-portfolio
  2. Click the green "Use this template" button
  3. Click "Create a new repository"
  4. Name it whatever you like (e.g. socsim-portfolio)
  5. Set it to Public
  6. Click "Create repository"


STEP 2 — Enable GitHub Pages
  1. In your new repo, click "Settings" (top tab)
  2. In the left sidebar, click "Pages"
  3. Under "Build and deployment":
     - Source: GitHub Actions
  4. Wait about 60 seconds, then your site will be live at:
     https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/YOUR-REPO-NAME/


STEP 3 — Create a Personal Access Token (PAT)
  This gives the editor permission to save your work directly
  to your repo. You only do this once.

  1. Go to: github.com/settings/tokens
  2. Click "Generate new token" → "Generate new token (classic)"
  3. Give it a name like: SOCSIM Portfolio Editor
  4. Set Expiration to 90 days (or "No expiration" if you prefer)
  5. Under "Select scopes", tick the box next to: repo
  6. Scroll down, click "Generate token"
  7. COPY the token now — GitHub only shows it once.
     It will look something like: ghp_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx


STEP 4 — Open your editor and connect it to your repo
  1. Go to: https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/YOUR-REPO-NAME/editor.html
  2. Click the "⚙ GitHub Settings" panel at the top
  3. Fill in:
       GitHub Username:       your GitHub username
       Repository Name:       your repo name (e.g. socsim-portfolio)
       Personal Access Token: paste the token from Step 3
  4. Click "Save settings"
  5. Click "⬇ Load from GitHub" to pull in your current data

  You're now connected — every button below needs this done first.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 2 — FILLING IN YOUR PROFILE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

After loading, scroll to the Profile section and fill in:

  Full Name        Your real name
  Role / Title     e.g. Security Analyst · Blue Team
  Tagline          One sentence about your focus and goals
  Location         City, Country
  Photo URL        Optional — a direct link to a profile photo
  LinkedIn         Your LinkedIn profile URL
  GitHub           Your GitHub profile URL
  Email            Your email address (optional)

Scroll down to the Theme section and pick one that suits you:
  Operator  — dark charcoal and orange (default)
  Terminal  — pure black and neon green
  Midnight  — deep navy and sky blue
  Obsidian  — dark with violet
  Arctic    — clean white and blue
  Crimson   — dark with red

Click "⬆ Publish to GitHub" when done.
Your profile goes live in about 60 seconds.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 3 — ADDING INVESTIGATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every time you complete an investigation in the SOCSIM lab,
add it to your portfolio:

  1. Open your editor.
  2. Click "⬇ Load from GitHub"
     This loads all your existing investigations so you do not
     overwrite anything.
  3. Scroll down to the relevant section:
     — Investigations        →  click "+ Add Investigation"
     — BEC Case Drops        →  click "+ Add BEC Case Drop"
  4. Fill in the fields (see below — same order as the form).
  5. Click "⬆ Publish to GitHub"
     Your site updates in about 60 seconds.

Note: each case's Case ID may only contain letters, numbers,
dots, dashes and underscores (it becomes part of a URL). The
editor blocks publishing if a Case ID breaks this rule.


INVESTIGATION FIELDS — in the same order as the form

  An investigation can cover a single alert, a whole Defender
  incident, or activity you found through hunting. Only the top
  fields are structured; everything else is free-form, and any
  section you leave empty is hidden on your site.

  Case details
    Case ID              Unique code. Suggested format: INV-001, INV-002…
    Date Investigated    When you investigated it.
    Severity              Critical / High / Medium / Low
    Verdict               True Positive / Benign True Positive / False Positive
    Title                 A short description of what you investigated.
    Reference             Optional. e.g. the Defender incident or alert name.

  Summary
    Two or three plain-English sentences — shown on your homepage
    card. Easiest to write last.

  Affected Assets
    Click "+ Add Asset" for each device, server, user or service
    account, or mailbox involved. Add an optional note, e.g.
    "Compromised — finance user's laptop".

  What Happened
    Your main write-up: what was run, by which account, on which
    device, what it does in plain English, and why the attacker
    did it. Mention the alerts here however you like.

  Timeline
    Click "+ Add Timeline Entry" for each malicious or suspicious
    event, in order — whether Defender alerted on it or not.
    Time (UTC) · Activity · Detail (optional, Markdown — wrap
    commands in `backticks`).

  Investigation & Queries
    Investigation   Pivots, hunting and leads you followed — what
                    you found, and what you ruled out.
    Queries         Paste any KQL you used (line breaks preserved).

  Impact, Verdict & Response
    Impact                      What was affected; did it succeed, or was
                                it blocked / prevented / detected / remediated?
    Verdict Justification       Why you reached your verdict.
    Response & Recommendations  Escalation, containment, remediation, and
                                anything that should be detected next time.

  Lessons Learned & Tools
    Lessons Learned   What you would do differently next time.
    Tools Used         One tool per line (e.g. Microsoft Defender XDR).

  Custom Sections
    Optional. Add your own headings — e.g. "MITRE ATT&CK Mapping",
    "IOCs" or "References" — with Markdown content.

  SOCSIM Verified
    Tick this if the investigation was done in the live lab.


BEC CASE DROP FIELDS — grouped exactly as the form shows them

  Case details
    Case ID             Unique code. Suggested format: BEC-001, BEC-002…
    Case Reference       The WDLabs reference number, if provided.
    Incident From / To   When the incident started and ended.
    Case Name             Descriptive title for the case.
    Affected Account     The compromised email account.
    Investigation Date   When you investigated it.
    PDF Report URL        Optional. If you have a PDF version of your report,
                          upload it to your repo (see Section 5) and paste
                          the URL here.

  Attack Narrative
    Plain-English summary of what happened. Write this last, as if
    explaining to a non-technical manager.

  Timeline of Events (UTC throughout)
    Each entry has:
      Time      e.g. 02:14 UTC
      Activity  Short label (e.g. "Threat actor signs in")
      Detail    Longer description of what happened.

  Findings
    3.1 — Initial Access          How did the threat actor get in?
    3.2 — Unauthorised Activity   What did they do once inside?
    3.3 — Data Access             What data was accessed or exfiltrated?

  Lessons Learned & Tools
    Lessons Learned   What you would approach differently.
    Tools Used         One tool per line.

  Custom Sections
    Optional. Add your own headings with Markdown content.

  SOCSIM Verified
    Tick this if done in the live lab.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 4 — FORMATTING YOUR WRITE-UPS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All the main narrative fields (Why it triggered, What Happened,
Evidence, Verdict Justification, Containment, Lessons Learned,
Attack Narrative, Initial Access, Unauthorised Activity, Data
Access) support Markdown via a toolbar — you do not need to know
Markdown syntax.

Toolbar buttons:
  B         Bold text
  I         Italic text
  ≡         Bullet list
  1.        Numbered list
  < >       Inline code (good for command names, IPs, filenames)
  📷        Upload screenshot (see Section 5)
  Image     Insert an image from a URL
  Link      Insert a hyperlink
  Eye       Preview — see how it will look on your portfolio

Useful shortcuts you can type directly:
  **bold text**          makes text bold
  `code or IP address`   formats as code
  - item                 bullet list item
  1. item                numbered list item


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 5 — ADDING SCREENSHOTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Screenshots make your write-ups much more compelling.

EASIEST WAY — the camera button (recommended)
  1. Click into the write-up field where you want the screenshot.
  2. Click the camera icon (📷) in the toolbar above the field.
  3. Pick your image file (.png / .jpg, up to 10 MB).
  4. Done — the editor uploads it to your repo and inserts it
     into your write-up automatically.

  (This needs your GitHub settings saved — the same one-time
  setup you did in Section 1.)

MANUAL WAY — if you prefer doing it yourself

  1. Take your screenshot and save it as a .png or .jpg file.

  2. Go to your GitHub repo in a browser.

  3. Navigate to the folder: src/assets/
     (Create this folder if it does not exist — click
     "Add file" → "Create new file", name it src/assets/.gitkeep,
     commit it.)

  4. Inside src/assets/, click "Add file" → "Upload files".
     Drag your screenshot in and click "Commit changes".

  5. Click on the uploaded file to open it.
     Click the "Raw" button.
     Copy the URL from your browser address bar.
     It will look like:
     https://raw.githubusercontent.com/USERNAME/REPO/main/src/assets/screenshot.png

  6. In your editor, paste it into any narrative field like this:
     ![Description of what the screenshot shows](paste-url-here)

The screenshot will appear in your published portfolio.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 6 — BACKING UP YOUR WORK
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Two buttons at the top of the editor protect your work:

  ↓ Backup       Downloads a portfolio.json file with everything
                 currently in the form. Keep this somewhere safe
                 before making big changes.
  ↑ Load file    Loads a portfolio.json file from your computer
                 back into the editor (e.g. to restore a backup).

Neither of these touches GitHub — they only move data between
the editor and your computer.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 7 — UPDATING YOUR PORTFOLIO SITE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every time the SOCSIM template is updated with new investigation
types, improved styling, or new features, you can pull those
updates in without touching your data.

  1. Open your editor and make sure your GitHub Settings are
     saved (Section 1, Step 4) — the update button needs this.
  2. Click "↻ Update template" in the header.
  3. Confirm the dialog — your investigations are safe.
  4. The update runs automatically in the background.
  5. Your site rebuilds in about 60 seconds with the new features.

Your portfolio.json (your profile and all your investigations)
is NEVER touched by the update. Only the site code changes.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CASE ID NAMING CONVENTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Use this format to keep IDs consistent:

  Investigations:         INV-001, INV-002, INV-003 ...
  (Older ALT- IDs carry over unchanged.)
  BEC case drops:         BEC-001, BEC-002, BEC-003 ...

This keeps your portfolio sortable and compatible with the
future central SOCSIM member directory.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ALREADY HAVE ALERT WRITE-UPS? (existing members)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Alert Investigations" are now "Investigations". When you update
your template, nothing is lost:

  - Your site shows your existing write-ups straight away.
  - The next time you open the editor, each alert write-up is
    converted to an investigation. Every field you wrote is kept:
    the alert itself becomes the first timeline entry, your steps
    and evidence move into "Investigation", and escalation /
    containment / tuning move into "Response & Recommendations".
  - Review them, then click Publish to save the new format.
  - Old links (…/alerts/ALT-001/) keep working — they redirect to
    the new page.
